How to read this page

These are lists, not rankings. Each journal is compared with this one manuscript, among the journals this consultation found. A place in a list says nothing outside it, and the three lists are not comparable with each other.

Merit and logistics are kept apart. Merit is what makes a journal a good home for the argument; logistics is cost, access and speed. A journal with fewer than two reasons of merit is marked thin on merit, however well it does on logistics.

The subject scores measure concentration, not suitability. They say how much of what a journal publishes is on your topics, so they favour narrow journals over broad ones that would also take the paper. Subject match is one number made of two of them — {{ topic_weight|round|int }} × topic + subfield, scaled to 1 — and is what the list by subject is ordered by. None of these has been validated against real outcomes: use them to read the list, not to decide.

Reordering never mixes the lists. «Order each list by» sorts inside each list. The lists were found in different ways, so one journal being above another only means something within a list.

{% if nr and not nr.skipped %}

The lists by reference describe your bibliography, not your argument. If most of your references are about the paper's subject, most of those journals will be too — which is what the smaller groups are there to correct. Small specialist journals come first here for the same reason they do by subject.

{% endif %}

A missing fact never removes a journal. When a journal's fee or access model is unknown, a constraint marks it for checking instead of excluding it.
