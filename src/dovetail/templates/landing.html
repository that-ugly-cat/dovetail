Dovetail

Where to send a paper. Paste a title, an abstract and your reference list, and Dovetail lists candidate journals — each with the reasons that hold it up, and with what it could not check said plainly. It finds and explains. You decide.

How it works

  1. 1
    You give it the manuscript Title and abstract, and the reference list if you have it. Optionally a word count, your funder, the highest fee you can pay.
  2. 2
    It reads what the paper is about OpenAlex classifies the title and abstract into topics. You are shown them, so you can see at once if the paper was misread.
  3. 3
    It looks for journals in two ways
    By subject Journals that publish on those topics, compared with your abstract at three levels of detail.
    By your references Journals whose recent papers cite the works you cite. This is how it finds the journals of your argument when the abstract names only its subject.
  4. 4
    It weighs each journal What makes a journal a good home for the argument is kept apart from fee, access and speed. Your constraints mark or exclude. A fact that is missing never excludes.
  5. 5
    You read the lists Separate lists, each with its reasons, that you can reorder. If you want, a model checks whether each journal publishes your kind of paper, and the word limits in its guidelines are compared with yours.

A consultation takes one to two minutes, and only you can see it.

What it holds to

≠
A list, not a ranking Each journal is compared with your manuscript, among the journals found that day. A place in a list says nothing about the journal in general, and the lists are never merged into one.
⚖
Merit and logistics apart A journal that does well only on cost, access and speed is marked. That rule comes from a real choice that rested on three logistical reasons and one about merit — and it was the one about merit that failed.
?
Unknown is not excluded The fee is missing for nine journals in ten. A journal is never removed for a fact nobody has: it stays, marked for you to check. And a journal nothing is known about is listed apart, not last.
⚿
Your manuscript stays yours Journals are shared by everyone. A consultation is seen only by whoever started it — not by other members and not by admins — and runs on your own keys, so it spends nobody else's budget. You can delete it.
⌘
Assistants are users too An MCP endpoint lets an AI assistant run a consultation and explain it, on your key and for you alone. It can propose a correction to a journal's record. It cannot approve one: that is a button, pressed by a person.
plain words Why choosing a journal needs this much care

The problem. Most papers go to the journal their authors thought of first: the one they read, the one a colleague suggested, the one with the name. When it is the wrong one, the answer comes back in a few days — «out of scope», or «not in a format we publish» — and the weeks spent formatting for it are gone. Then the second choice is made the same way.

What Dovetail does about it. It looks for candidates in two ways that do not depend on each other. One reads your abstract and asks which journals publish on that subject. The other reads your bibliography and asks which journals are citing the same works you cite — which is where your argument is already being discussed, whatever its subject. A journal both ways lead to is a strong candidate. When the two disagree, the disagreement is itself the finding: it usually means the paper is about one thing and argues in another field.

Why it shows its reasons. A bare list of names would be one more opinion. Each journal comes with what holds it up, split in two: reasons of merit, and practical ones. People choosing a journal tend to be convinced by the second kind — it is fast, it is open, it is cheap — so a journal that has only those is flagged.

What it does not do. It does not predict acceptance and it does not rank journals. Its scores say how concentrated a journal is on your subject, which favours narrow journals over broad ones that would also take the paper; they have not been checked against real outcomes. It reads only references that have a DOI, so a bibliography of books tells it little. It is a way to find candidates you would not have thought of and to see why each is there. The reading is yours.

geek mode Data, scoring, models, MCP surface

Stack. FastAPI, SQLite in WAL mode, Jinja2, Docker behind Caddy and Borant ID. No JavaScript framework: three small scripts, for the theme, for forms in flight and for reordering a list.

Data. OpenAlex for the inventory — journals, their topics, works and citations — and DOAJ for licence, fees and declared time to publication. Every field on a journal's record carries its own source and date, and one older than 180 days is checked again before it is trusted to exclude.

By subject. /text/topics classifies title and abstract; journals sharing a topic are swept, and each is scored by the cosine between the text's topic vector and the journal's, at topic, subfield and field level. The list is ordered by 2 × topic + subfield. A journal is reachable this way only if the topic is among its 25 most frequent, which is the limit the second road exists for.

By reference. For each DOI in the reference list, the works citing it since 1 January five years back, grouped by journal — one call per reference, so that breadth can be counted and one heavily cited review does not become the list. A journal passes when at least 5% of the references lead to it, and never fewer than two; the order is citation links per 1,000 works it published in the same window. The references are also read one field at a time, and each minority field gets up to three journals of its own. Nothing is merged with the subject score.

Models. Two optional steps, each on the user's own Anthropic key. {{ genre_model }} reads a journal's recent titles and judges form and method, not subject; its verdict is stored beside the scores and never orders anything, because it is not reproducible. {{ guidelines_model }} reads one page of author guidelines into article types, and every number is kept only if the sentence it was read from is on the page and the number is in the sentence.

Whose is what. Journals are one shared table. A consultation carries its owner, and one that is not yours answers 404 like one that does not exist, on every surface. OpenAlex and Anthropic keys are per user, Fernet-encrypted, and each personal OpenAlex key spends from a ledger of its own.
