Five to ten is the right size, taken from different years. Ten articles from the same special issue describe that issue, not the journal — and the profile they produce would then send the wrong papers here for as long as nobody rebuilds it.

The coverage figure stays empty, deliberately. On a journal indexed by OpenAlex it means «how much of the output these topics cover», and here the sample is everything that is known — calling that coverage would dress an unmeasured thing as a measurement.

A profile built this way starts at a disadvantage the score does not correct for: the cosine is invariant to scale but not to shape, so a handful of articles gives a flat vector, and a flat vector scores low against a pointed manuscript. It measures how concentrated a journal looks, and a small sample never looks concentrated.
